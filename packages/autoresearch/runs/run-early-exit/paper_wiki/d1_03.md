## Abstract

In LLM inference, early-exit stops computation at an intermediate layer once the prediction is sufficiently confident, reducing latency and cost. But modern LLMs use improved pretraining recipes and architectures that reduce layer redundancy, potentially limiting early-exit opportunities. The authors re-evaluate layer-wise early-exit in modern LLMs, analyze how intermediate representations evolve during training, introduce a metric for intrinsic early-exit suitability, and propose a benchmark for exploring early-exit benefits on different models/workloads. They find a diminishing trend in early-exit effectiveness across newer generations; dense transformers generally offer more early-exit potential than Mixture-of-Experts and State Space Models; larger (>20B) and base pretrained models show higher early-exit potential.

## Abstract

Post-training quantization cuts cost but fundamentally alters social biases in ways aggregate metrics miss. The authors present the first large-scale study of 50 quantized models on PostTrainingBiasBench, a unified benchmark of 13 closed- and open-ended bias datasets. They identify 'quantization-induced masked bias flipping', where up to 21% of responses flip between biased and unbiased states after quantization despite no change in aggregate bias scores. Flips are strongly driven by model uncertainty: high-uncertainty responses are 3-11x more likely to change than confident ones. Quantization strength amplifies this — 4-bit models show 4-6x more behavioral change than 8-bit. Critically, changes create asymmetric impacts across demographic groups, where bias can worsen by up to 18.6% for some groups while improving by 14.1% for others, yielding misleadingly neutral aggregate outcomes. Larger models show no consistent robustness advantage, and group-specific shifts vary unpredictably across model families.

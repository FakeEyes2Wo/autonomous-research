## Abstract

LLMs are large and costly/slow to run, so a body of work proposes inference-efficiency strategies — quantization, pruning, and caching — that cut cost/latency by several factors while retaining much of benchmark accuracy. This work explores another performance dimension: demographic bias in model generations due to inference-acceleration optimizations. Using a wide range of metrics, they probe bias from many angles. Analysis before/after acceleration shows significant change in bias. Worryingly, these bias effects are complex and unpredictable: a given acceleration-strategy × bias-type combination may show little change in one model but a large effect in another. The results highlight a need for case-by-case evaluation of model bias after acceleration changes.

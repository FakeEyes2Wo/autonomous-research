## One-liner

Introduces temperature scaling as a simple, effective post-hoc calibration method for modern neural nets.

## Key Finding

Modern networks are overconfident (poor ECE), and a single temperature parameter learned on validation data substantially recalibrates them, better than Platt or binning in many settings.

## Implications / Insights

Confidence scores are systematically miscalibrated, so any threshold on confidence (e.g., early exit) inherits that miscalibration.

## Relevance to Us

Grounds the calibration-disparity mechanism: if temperature/confidence reliability differs across groups, a shared exit threshold exits at different true-error levels.

## Abstract

Shows that modern neural networks are typically overconfident and miscalibrated, and introduces temperature scaling — a simple post-hoc transform of logits that markedly improves calibration.

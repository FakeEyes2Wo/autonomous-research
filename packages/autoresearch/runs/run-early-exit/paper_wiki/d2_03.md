## Abstract

LLMs serve heterogeneous populations structured by domain, topic difficulty, and linguistic style. Conformal risk control (CRC) gives rigorous marginal risk guarantees for selective prediction with abstention, but marginal guarantees do not imply per-group ones: a model can meet the population budget while systematically over-exposing subgroups to errors. Under mild shifts in group composition, standard CRC violates the budget in up to 47% of trials. HG-CRC (Hierarchical Group-Conditional CRC) is a post-hoc calibration framework enforcing simultaneous risk guarantees across all nodes of a user-defined group hierarchy, using a Bonferroni correction over nodes and a leaf-first policy that uses the most specific applicable threshold, backing off to coarser nodes when a finer one is uncertified or rejects the example. It needs only a held-out calibration set, no retraining. Evaluated on Qwen3-4B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Gemma-3-4B over ARC Challenge and MMLU-Pro across eight configurations (IID, heterogeneity, mixture/domain/prompt/difficulty shift, label noise, quantization), HG-CRC reaches an empirical 0% violation rate and WGER=0 on ARC for high-accuracy models; results are benchmark-specific (MMLU-Pro models abstain entirely or retain WGER=0.014). Participation cost vs. global CRC is 22-37 points. Ablations show hierarchical depth clears the budget (removing difficulty level returns violations to ~11%); Bonferroni is needed for the guarantee though its empirical effect matters only with many nodes.

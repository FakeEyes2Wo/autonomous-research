## Abstract

The high memory demands of generative LLMs motivate quantization, which reduces memory by mapping weights to lower-precision integers. Recent empirical work shows quantization can increase the likelihood of biased outputs and degrade fairness benchmarks. This work links quantization and fairness by adding explicit group-fairness constraints to the quantization objective and introduces Fair-GPTQ, the first quantization method explicitly designed to reduce unfairness in LLMs. The added constraints guide the rounding operation toward less-biased text generation for protected groups, focusing on stereotype generation involving occupational bias and discriminatory language across gender, race, and religion. Fair-GPTQ has minimal accuracy impact (retains at least 90% of baseline accuracy on zero-shot benchmarks), reduces unfairness relative to a half-precision model, and preserves the memory/speed benefits of 4-bit quantization.

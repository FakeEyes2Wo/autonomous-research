## One-liner

Magnitude pruning disproportionately hurts minority subgroups' accuracy, so the compressed model is less fair than its dense counterpart.

## Key Finding

Aggressive pruning produces unequal accuracy loss across demographics, with underrepresented groups bearing a larger drop, even when overall (average) accuracy is preserved.

## Implications / Insights

An efficiency transform that looks 'fair' on mean accuracy can be systematically unfair to minority groups, so compression must be audited per-group.

## Relevance to Us

Directly the closest analog to early-exit LLMs: an efficiency transform preserving average quality but redistributing error onto minority groups, motivating subgroup-level auditing of dynamic exits.

## Abstract

Shows that standard magnitude-based pruning degrades accuracy far more severely for minority/underrepresented groups than for majority groups, so compression introduces or amplifies group disparity; proposes a constrained pruning formulation.

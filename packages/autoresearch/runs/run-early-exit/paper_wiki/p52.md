## One-liner

LLM verbalized confidence exhibits substantial overconfidence and poor failure prediction, casting doubt on its reliability.

## Key Finding

Vanilla verbalized confidence is overconfident and unreliable for detecting wrong answers; certain prompting/self-consistency methods improve uncertainty estimation but not to a reliable degree.

## Implications / Insights

Since LLM confidence is unreliable, any confidence-driven early-exit decision has an uncertain basis, which could differ by input/group.

## Relevance to Us

Directly supports the calibration-disparity hypothesis: if confidence reliability differs across groups, confidence-based early exit yields unequal quality.

## Abstract

Empirically evaluates how well LLMs can express uncertainty (verbalized confidence, sampling-based, verbalized-probability), finding significant overconfidence and poor failure prediction in vanilla confidence elicitation.

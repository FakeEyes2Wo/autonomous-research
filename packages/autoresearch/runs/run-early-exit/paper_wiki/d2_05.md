## Abstract

Hallucination detection is increasingly framed as selective prediction: an estimator scores confidence and abstains when low. Unsupervised sampling detectors (Semantic Entropy) avoid labels but plateau in quality, while supervised probes are strong in-distribution but degrade under calibration-label scarcity. The authors recover the response manifold as the density ridge of a kernel density estimate over a six-dimensional kinematic feature map of hidden-state generation trajectories, scoring a test generation by the negated Euclidean distance from its projected point to the nearest ridge vertex. Against Semantic Entropy, topological methods, and log-probability on six QA benchmarks (HaluEval-QA, TriviaQA, GSM8K, POPE, ScienceQA, A-OKVQA) with eight text/vision LLMs under a label-scarce protocol (n_cal=200, N=5 generations), the ridge score beats on AUROC by 5-20 points with tempered degradation under label scarcity.
